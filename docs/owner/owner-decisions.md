# Your decisions

Some things are not Serva's to decide. Money, policy, legal commitments, what the product
is for, what you are willing to trade away. When it reaches one of those it stops and asks.

## What a question looks like

Every one arrives with:

- **the question**, in plain language
- **why Serva cannot decide it** — if that reason does not convince you, say so; a question
  Serva should have answered itself is a defect
- **what Serva recommends**, and the reasoning behind it
- **what each option would mean**

## Two kinds

**Choose one.** Serva offers options and you pick one of them. Free text is refused here,
because an answer nothing can act on is not an answer.

**Give a value.** A number, a date, a duration, a name. Serva needs the actual value, not a
choice about the value — and it will refuse a meta-answer like "decide it later" where a
value was asked for.

## What answering means

Your answer becomes project truth. Departments proceed on it, later work cites it, and the
history records it with your name and the time.

You can change your mind. That is a change request, and Serva will show you what it affects
before anything moves.

## What cannot answer for you

No agent can. Serva refuses an answer attributed to one — at the point of writing, and again
at every point of reading. A company that can grant itself permission is not a governed
company, and the refusal is the difference.
